The price of a website in Morocco depends on seven factors: the type of site, the design (template or custom), the content, the number of languages, the features, the level of SEO included and maintenance. That is why two quotes for "a showcase website" can be far apart: they do not describe the same work. This guide helps you understand what you are paying for, anticipate the costs that come back every year, and compare two quotes without getting it wrong.

## Why do prices vary so much?

A website is not a standard product. The same word hides very different things: a template bought and adapted in a few days, or a site where every page was thought through, written, designed and developed for your business. Both are called "a showcase website". The first costs little to produce, the second takes weeks of work.

So the useful question is not "how much does a website cost?" but "what is included in this price?".

## The 7 factors that set the price of a website

### 1. The type of site

| Type | What it is for | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page | A single page for one offer or ad campaign | Low |
| Showcase site | Presenting the business and its services, generating enquiries | Medium |
| Site with a blog | Showcase plus regular articles for SEO | Medium to high |
| E-commerce site | Catalogue, cart, online payment, order management | High |
| Custom platform | Booking, client area, business tools | High to very high |

### 2. The design: template or custom

A template is a ready-made design shared by many websites. It is quick and cheap, but your site will look like others. A custom design starts from your brand, your customers and your goals: it stands out, but takes real design work.

### 3. The content: copy and photos

Who writes the copy? Who supplies the photos? A quote that assumes you provide all the content is not comparable to one that includes copywriting and a photo shoot. Content is also what makes a site convert and rank on Google.

### 4. The number of languages

In Morocco, a site in French, Arabic and sometimes English is common. Careful: Arabic is not just a translation. It is written right to left, which requires a mirrored layout, suitable Arabic typography and dedicated testing. Each language also adds pages to write and optimise.

### 5. The features

Contact form, WhatsApp button, appointment booking, client area, CRM integration, online payment. To take card payments in Morocco, the site has to be connected to a payment gateway, such as the CMI's, which involves steps with your bank and specific development. Every feature adds development and testing.

### 6. The SEO included

This is one of the most common gaps between two quotes. A site can be delivered with no SEO groundwork at all, or with solid foundations: a title and description per page, a clean heading structure, fast pages, a sitemap, structured data, and a site that reads perfectly on mobile. These basics cost little to build in during creation, and much more to fix afterwards.

### 7. Maintenance and changes

A website lives: security updates, backups, fixes, new pages. Some quotes include this for a period, others bill it separately, others do not mention it.

## The recurring costs to plan for every year

The build is not the only expense. Always ask what will come back each year:

| Item | What it is | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Domain name | Your address (.ma, .com...), renewed every year | That it is registered in your name |
| Hosting | The server that runs the site | Performance, backups, SSL certificate included |
| Licences | Any paid themes, plugins or tools | Who pays for renewal |
| Maintenance | Updates, security, small changes | What is included, and response time |

## How do you compare two quotes?

Put the quotes side by side and check every line:

| Does the quote specify... | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| The number and list of pages | Avoids surprises mid-project |
| Template or custom design | This is the main price gap |
| Who writes the copy and supplies the photos | Content is a large share of the work |
| The languages included, Arabic in particular | Arabic needs a specific layout |
| The features, one by one | WhatsApp, form, booking, payment... |
| The SEO basics included | Titles, descriptions, speed, sitemap, mobile |
| The yearly costs | Domain, hosting, licences, maintenance |
| The schedule and number of revisions | A project without stages drifts |
| Ownership of the site and access | See the next section |

## Who owns your website? The question to ask before signing

This is the most often overlooked point, and the most expensive one in a dispute:

- **The domain name** must be registered in your name, not the provider's.
- **You must receive full access**: site administration, hosting, and the code if the site is custom-developed.
- **The content** (copy, photos, logo) produced for you must belong to you.

A site whose domain belongs to the provider makes you dependent on them, even after the relationship ends.

## Warning signs in a quote

- A very low price with no detail of pages or features.
- No mention of SEO, speed or mobile display.
- A domain registered in the provider's name.
- No schedule, no limit on revisions.
- A template presented as "custom": ask to see other sites they have built.

One last legal point: in Morocco, Law 09-08 governs the collection of personal data, under the supervision of the CNDP. A site that collects names, phone numbers or emails through a form must tell visitors how that data is used. A serious provider raises this with you.
